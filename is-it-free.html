Is WinSCP free? Licence, Microsoft Store price and donations

Yes, WinSCP is free. The full program costs nothing to download or use, at home or at work. This page explains the open source licence, what the $19.99 Microsoft Store copy is, the donation amounts, and what the vendor does not publish.

WinSCP is free and open source. Every feature is in the standard download, with no trial and no paid tier. The vendor also sells the same program in Microsoft Store for $19.99 and accepts donations from $19 to $249; both are optional ways to fund development. The pages checked publish no refund policy.

Is WinSCP free for personal and business use?

Is WinSCP free? Yes, for everyone. The program is released under the GNU General Public License, which lets anyone run it for any purpose at no charge. That covers individuals, schools, small firms and large companies alike. You do not have to register, accept a trial or enter a key.

Companies often ask anyway, because their software policy wants a licence count. The vendor's FAQ has an entry for exactly that question. With an open source licence there is nothing to count. What your policy may still care about is where the installer comes from, so take it from the official download page and not from a mirror.

There are three ways to get the program or pay for it. None of them changes what the program can do.

What the Microsoft Store copy is

The install guide lists Microsoft Store as a second source at $19.99. The vendor's Store page says the Store copy is built from the same files as the classic installer and suggests buying it as a form of donation. Some people prefer it because the Store installs updates on its own and keeps a purchase record that an expense report can use.

It adds no features. If you have already installed the free version, buying the Store copy gives you nothing new apart from Store updates and the feeling of having paid for a tool you use every day.

Donations

Most documentation pages show a Donate box with five amounts: $19, $29, $49, $99 and $249. A donation unlocks nothing; it pays for continued development. The vendor points out that releases have come every one to three months since 2000, and that the developers have answered the support forum since 2002.

What the vendor does not publish

Because the program is free, several things that pricing pages usually list are missing. Before you buy the Store copy or donate, note the following:

  • No refund policy appears on the pages checked. A Store purchase is handled by Microsoft under its own terms.
  • No paid support contract or service level is listed. Support is the public forum.
  • No enterprise or volume edition exists, so there is no price list for one.
  • No coupon or discount is listed, and none is needed for a free program.

Is it worth paying for?

You never have to pay. Buy the Store copy or donate if the program saves you time and you want it to keep getting updates. If you are comparing it with paid tools, note that FileZilla keeps S3 and WebDAV for its paid Pro edition; the WinSCP and FileZilla comparison has the details. For a judgement on quality rather than price, read the review. If you need a client on another platform, see the alternatives.

FAQ

Can I use WinSCP at work for free?

Yes. The GNU General Public License lets anyone run the program for any purpose, including commercial work, without paying. There is no business edition and no per seat fee.

What does the $19.99 Microsoft Store version add?

No features. The vendor describes it as the same program and suggests the purchase as a way to donate. The only practical difference is that the Store installs updates for you.

Is there a WinSCP coupon or discount?

No coupon is needed, because the program is free. The only paid option is the Store copy at a fixed $19.99, and the vendor pages checked list no discounts.

Can I get a refund for the Store purchase?

The vendor's pages checked here do not publish a refund policy. The purchase goes through Microsoft, so any request falls under Microsoft's Store terms.

Do donations unlock anything?

No. Every feature is already in the free download. Donations from $19 to $249 only fund development.

Is the .NET assembly free too?

Yes. It ships in the same free download. It has its own licence terms, so check the licence page before you bundle it with software you distribute.
